A structured, step-by-step roadmap to construct your first language from scratch, designed without assuming formal linguistic training.

## Glossary of Plain-Language Terminology

Before starting, keep these core terms handy:

- **Conlang (Constructed Language):** An intentionally created language, rather than one evolved naturally (like English or Spanish).
- **Phonetics & Phonology:** The sound system. Phonetics is the physical sound made; phonology is the set of meaningful sounds your language chooses to use.
- **Phoneme:** A single distinct sound unit that changes word meaning (e.g., the /p/ vs /b/ sound in *pat* vs *bat*).
- **Phonotactics:** The rules governing allowed sound combinations and syllable shapes (e.g., English allows "str-" at the start of a syllable, Hawaiian does not).
- **Morphology:** How words are built, combined, or modified (e.g., adding *-ed* for past tense, or *-s* for plural).
- **Syntax:** Sentence structure and word order (e.g., Subject-Verb-Object vs Subject-Object-Verb).
- **Orthography:** The writing system and spelling rules.
- **Lexicon:** The dictionary / vocabulary list of the language.

### Phase 1: Purpose & Vision
Define the scope and world of your language.

- **Conlang Type:**
  - *Artlang (Artistic Language):* Designed for worldbuilding, literature, or aesthetic pleasure (e.g., Elvish, Dothraki).
  - *Auxlang (Auxiliary Language):* Designed for simple, universal human communication (e.g., Esperanto).
  - *Engineered/Logical Language:* Designed to test philosophical ideas or logic (e.g., Lojban).
- **Vibe & Constraints:**
  - Harsh, guttural sounds or soft, melodic flow?
  - Who speaks it? Under what climate, culture, or societal structure?

---

### Phase 2: Phonology (Sounds & Syllables)
Establish the phonetic palette and allowed shapes.

1. **Consonants & Vowels:**
   - Pick 15–25 consonants and 3–7 vowels.
   - Example 5-vowel system (common worldwide): `a`, `e`, `i`, `o`, `u`.
2. **Syllable Structure (Phonotactics):**
   - Define allowed syllable patterns using `C` (Consonant) and `V` (Vowel).
   - Strict CV: *ka, to, mi* (Japanese-like).
   - Moderate CVC: *kat, tom, mik*.
   - Complex CCVCC: *plant, street*.
3. **Stress Rules:**
   - Where does emphasis fall in multi-syllable words?
   - Fixed stress: always on the first syllable, penult (second-to-last), or last syllable.

---

### Phase 3: Morphology (Word Structures)
Decide how words change form to convey grammar.

1. **Typological Style:**
   - *Isolating:* Words never change form; grammatical particles indicate tense/plural (e.g., Mandarin).
   - *Agglutinative:* Words stack neat, distinct prefixes/suffixes like Lego bricks (e.g., Turkish, Swahili).
   - *Fusional:* One ending packs multiple meanings together (e.g., Latin, Russian).
2. **Core Noun Categories:**
   - Number: Singular vs Plural (or Dual for pairs).
   - Gender/Noun Class: Masculine/Feminine, or Animate/Inanimate, or None.
   - Case: Do nouns change based on their job in the sentence? (Subject, Direct Object, Possessor).
3. **Core Verb Categories:**
   - Tense: Past, Present, Future.
   - Aspect: Completed action vs Ongoing action.
   - Agreement: Does the verb change to match who is doing it? (I walk vs He walk**s**).

---

### Phase 4: Syntax (Sentence Rules)
Determine how clauses and sentences are ordered.

1. **Basic Word Order:**
   - Pick a default order:
     - **SVO** (Subject-Verb-Object): "She ate apples." (English, Spanish, Swahili)
     - **SOV** (Subject-Object-Verb): "She apples ate." (Japanese, Turkish, Latin)
     - **VSO** (Verb-Subject-Object): "Ate she apples." (Classical Arabic, Irish)
2. **Modifiers:**
   - Adjective + Noun ("red car") OR Noun + Adjective ("car red").
   - Prepositions ("in the box") OR Postpositions ("the box in").
3. **Questions & Negation:**
   - How do you ask a question? (Tone change, question particle, or inversion?)
   - How do you say "no" or negate a verb?

---

### Phase 5: Building the Lexicon (Vocabulary)
Do not translate an English dictionary A-to-Z. Build modular roots.

1. **Root Creation:**
   - Generate phonetic roots following Phase 2 rules.
2. **Derivational Affixes (Word Generators):**
   - Define reusable modifiers:
     - Person who does X: `verb + -er` (e.g., hunt -> hunter).
     - Place of X: `verb + -ry` (e.g., bake -> bakery).
     - State of being X: `adjective + -ness` (e.g., dark -> darkness).
3. **Swadesh 100 List:**
   - Start by translating universal core human concepts: body parts, basic nature (sun, water, fire), simple verbs (eat, sleep, see), numbers 1-10, pronouns.

---

### Phase 6: Orthography & First Text Test
Validate the grammar and sound system through real use.

1. **Romanization:**
   - Assign clear standard keyboard letters to your sounds before attempting custom scripts or alphabets.
2. **Translation Test:**
   - Translate a standard benchmark:
     - *The North Wind and the Sun* (Aesop's fable).
     - *Babel Text* (Genesis 11:1–9).
     - 5 everyday conversational sentences.
3. **Iterative Refinement:**
   - If a sentence sounds clunky or ambiguous, tweak the rules and update this document.
