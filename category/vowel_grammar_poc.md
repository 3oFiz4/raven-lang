# Proof of Concept: Vowel-Only Grammatical Markers

Testing the hypothesis: **Can pure vowels (`V`) serve exclusively as functional grammar markers (negation, tense, aspect, question)?**

---

## 1. Capacity & Productivity Check

- **Total Vowels in Inventory:** 14 distinct vowels.
- **Core Grammatical Roles Needed:**
  1. Negation: `i`
  2. Question / Interrogative
  3. Past Tense
  4. Future Tense
  5. Present / Habitual Aspect
  6. Continuous / Progressive Aspect
  7. Plural Marker
  8. Conjunction ("and")
  9. Disjunction ("or")
  10. Conditional ("if")

**Verdict on Productivity:** **Highly productive.** 14 vowels are more than enough to cover the core closed-class grammatical markers of an analytic language. It cleanly splits the language into two clear sound tiers:
- **`V` (vowel alone):** Functional grammar marker.
- **`CV` (consonant + vowel):** Lexical content root (nouns, verbs, adjectives).

---

## 2. Test Cases for Negation `i`

Assume test roots:
- `to` = water / liquid
- `pa` = good
- `ka` = eat
- `mi` = I / me

### Scenario A: Prefix-like Attachment (`i-` attached directly)
- `pa` (good) $\rightarrow$ `ipa` (bad / not good)
- `to` (water) $\rightarrow$ `ito` (dry / non-water)
- `ka` (eat) $\rightarrow$ `ika` (fast / not eat)

**Syllable Analysis:**
- Root: `CV`
- Negated: `V.CV` (`i.pa`, `i.to`, `i.ka`)
- **Compatibility:** Fully compliant with the `(C)V` canon. It consists of two open syllables: `V` + `CV`.

### Scenario B: Standalone Particle `i`
- `mi pa` = I [am] good.
- `mi i pa` = I [am] not good.
- `mi to ka` = I water consume.
- `mi to i ka` = I water do not consume.

---

## 3. The One Edge Case & Rule Fix

- **The Problem (Vowel Hiatus):**  
  If a content word has no onset consonant (a pure `V` root like `a`), prefixing `i` creates `ia` (two vowels touching). In speech, vowels either blend into a diphthong or cause a glottal stop stutter.
- **The Clean Solution:**  
  Reserve all 14 bare `V` slots **exclusively** for grammatical particles, and require all content roots (nouns, verbs, adjectives) to be `CV` (with an explicit consonant onset).
- **Result:**
  Every word boundary is crystal clear:
  `[Grammar V] + [Lexical CV] = [V.CV]` $\rightarrow$ zero vowel collisions ever.
